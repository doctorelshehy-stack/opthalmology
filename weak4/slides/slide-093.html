Ocular trauma · Mechanical Week 4 · Ophthalmology

Mechanical injuries

Foreign body,
sharp or blunt

Extraocular foreign bodies should be removed. Penetrating injuries may lacerate the cornea or lens and introduce infection; blunt injuries act by compression and decompression.

A · Extraocular foreign body

Either in the conjunctival sac or on the cornea. It should be removed.

B · Penetrating injuries

Causes:

  1. Sharp objects — knives, scissors, needles, etc.
  2. Flying foreign bodies — air-gun pellet, pieces of metal or glass.

Clinical effects:

  1. Corneal laceration — partial or full thickness.
  2. Penetrating injury into the lens.
  3. Intraocular foreign body → infection, chemical reaction.

C · Non-penetrating injuries

Causes:

  • Trauma by a blunt object — e.g. fist, stick, tennis ball or squash ball.

Mechanism:

Compression and decompression of the site of the globe.
