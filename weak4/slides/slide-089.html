Chemical injuries

The most urgent
of ocular emergencies

Common industrial and household accidents. Alkalis penetrate deeply and rapidly; acids produce more localised epithelial damage.

a · Acids

  • Sulphuric acid — as in toilet cleaner and battery fluids.
  • Sodium or calcium hypochloride — 70% in pool cleaners, or 30% in bleaches.

b · Alkalis

  • Potassium hydroxide in drain cleaner.
  • Sodium hydroxide in oven cleaner.
  • Lime (calcium monoxide) — the most serious, as it reacts with water to produce heat.
Alkalis damage ocular tissue rapidly, with deep penetration into the inside of the eyeball unless stopped by proper treatment.
Acids, on the other hand, cause more localised damage at the epithelial level.
