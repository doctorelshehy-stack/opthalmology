Abnormal pupillary responses · A and B

Absolute versus
relative afferent defect

A completely blind eye with no light perception has an amaurotic pupil. An incomplete optic nerve lesion produces a relative afferent pupillary defect.

A · Amaurotic pupil

Absolute afferent pupillary defect.

  • Causes: lesions that completely destroy the optic nerve.
  • The involved eye is blind (no PL).
  • Both pupils are equal in size — no anisocoria.
  • When the affected eye is stimulated, neither pupil reacts; when the normal eye is stimulated, both pupils constrict.
  • Accommodation is normal.

B · Marcus Gunn pupil

Relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD).

  • Causes: incomplete optic nerve lesion or severe retinal disease.
  • Variable decrease in visual acuity of the affected eye.
  • Both pupils are equal in size.
  • Swinging flashlight test: each pupil is stimulated in rapid succession. When the normal pupil is stimulated, both pupils constrict; when the abnormal pupil is stimulated, it appears to dilate paradoxically instead of constricting.
The RAPD is relative because the affected eye still perceives some light; the amaurotic pupil is the absolute end of the same afferent spectrum.
