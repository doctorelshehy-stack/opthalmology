Chemical injury · first aid

Time is
a vital factor

Immediate copious irrigation, removal of particulate matter, debridement of necrotic epithelium and pH testing are the first-aid priorities.

Clinical manifestations

  • Varies from mild to severe conjunctival hyperaemia and chemosis, corneal oedema and anterior chamber reaction.
  • In severe cases, corneal ulceration that ends by perforation may occur.
  • Cicatrizing complications may follow severe cases: conjunctival scarring, xerosis, symblepharon, trichiasis, entropion and corneal scarring.

1 · First aid — immediate treatment

  1. Copious irrigation of the eyes using running water, saline or Ringer's lactate solution for 20 minutes or by 2.00 litres.
  2. Any particulate foreign body of the chemical matter on the corneal surface, lids and fornices should be removed with cotton or forceps, followed by re-irrigation.
  3. Debridement of necrotic areas of corneal epithelium should be performed at the slit lamp to promote re-epithelialisation and remove associated chemical residue.
  4. If the nature of the chemical irritant is unknown, pH paper (litmus paper) is applied to the involved tissue to determine the chemical nature. It is also used to check the pH after irrigation — normal pH is 7.3–7.7.
