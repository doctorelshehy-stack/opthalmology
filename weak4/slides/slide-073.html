Definition

Drooping of the upper eyelid. Normally the upper lid covers the upper 1/6 of the cornea in the primary position.

A · Congenital — more common

  • Due to faulty development of the levator muscle or its nerve supply (levator dysgenesis, leading to poor contraction and incomplete relaxation).
  • Dated since birth; usually bilateral but may be unilateral.
  • May be associated with other anomalies:
  • Weak superior rectus — the superior rectus and levator develop from the same dermatome.
  • Other congenital anomalies — epicanthus and blepharophimosis.
  • Marcus Gunn phenomenon (jaw-winking): the ptosed lid elevates on moving the jaw due to faulty innervation.

The lid margin sits lower than the normal upper sixth of the cornea.
