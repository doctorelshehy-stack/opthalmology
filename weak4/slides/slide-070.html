A controlled
aperture

The pupil is a hole in the centre — slightly nasal — of the iris, 3–4 mm in diameter, controlled by two antagonistic involuntary smooth muscles.

Definition and size

A hole in the centre (slightly nasal) of the iris. Size: 3–4 mm.

Two antagonistic muscles

  • Sphincter (constrictor) pupillae: parasympathetic supply via the branch of the oculomotor nerve to the inferior oblique.
  • Dilator pupillae: sympathetic nerve supply.

Functions

  1. Regulation of the amount of light entering the eye.
  2. Diminishing aberrations of the image by cutting peripheral light rays, preventing them from passing through the periphery of the crystalline lens.

Both pupils are equal in size; diameter is controlled by opposing smooth muscles.
