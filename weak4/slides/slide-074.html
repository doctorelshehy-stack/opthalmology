Ptosis · acquired

Neurogenic, myogenic,
aponeurotic, mechanical

Acquired ptosis is less common than congenital ptosis but has many causes, grouped by mechanism.

1 · Neurogenic (paralytic)

  • 3rd nerve palsy — either compressive or vasculopathic.
  • Horner's syndrome (sympathetic ptosis): mild unilateral ptosis, miosis and anhidrosis, enophthalmos, flushing of the face and heterochromia iridis (in congenital cases).

2 · Myogenic

  • Due to disease of the levator muscle — trauma, inflammation, neoplasms, muscle dystrophy or myasthenia gravis.
  • In myasthenia gravis, ptosis is periodic, worst in the evening when the patient is tired and disappears in the morning.
  • Anticholinesterase drugs give rapid relief of ptosis in myasthenia gravis.

3 · Aponeurotic (senile / involutional)

Degenerative changes in the levator aponeurosis while levator function is good.

4 · Traumatic

Direct trauma to the levator muscle or its nerve supply (wounds or fracture).

5 · Mechanical

Due to increased weight of the lid from inflammatory infiltration, tumour, multiple chalazia, spring catarrh and blepharochalasis.

6 · Hysterical

Occurs in young females with emotional trouble.
