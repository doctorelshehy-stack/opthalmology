Retina · Vein occlusion

Branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO)

Symptoms
  • Peripheral branch vein occlusion may be asymptomatic.
  • Sudden diminution of vision due to macular edema.
  • Loss of part of field of vision.
Signs (Fig.12-10)
  • Dilated and tortuous veins at the area drained by obstructed vein.
  • Dot-blot and flame-shaped hemorrhages at the area drained by obstructed vein.
  • Retinal edema at the area drained by obstructed vein.
  • Cotton wool spots at the area drained by obstructed vein.
Complications
  • Chronic macular edema, the most common cause of persistent poor visual acuity.
  • Neovascularization of the retina that cause vitreous hemorrhage.
Treatment
  • Laser treatment for macular edema after resolution of hemorrhage.
  • Aspirin 75 mg /day to reduce platelet aggregation.
  • Treatment of predisposing factors such as systemic hypertension and diabetes.
