Pseudoptosis and the ptosis examination

The lid is low,
the muscles are normal

Apparent ptosis mimics true ptosis. True ptosis produces compensatory frontalis activity, head posture and, in long-standing cases, amblyopia and torticollis.

Apparent (pseudo) ptosis

The upper lid is abnormally low although the levator and Müller's muscle are normal.

Causes

  • Enophthalmos.
  • Loss of orbital fat in old age.
  • Atrophic eye or empty socket (lack of support).
  • Unilateral lid retraction.

Complications · especially unilateral

  1. Ocular torticollis due to contracture of the sternomastoid muscle.
  2. Amblyopia and squint in long-standing cases from occlusion and disuse.

Symptoms

  • Bad cosmetic appearance.
  • Interference with vision, if the pupil is covered.

Signs

  • Drooping of the upper eyelid and absence of the lid crease.
  • In severe cases, compensatory changes:
  • The frontalis contracts to elevate the lid, causing corrugations and wrinkling of the forehead and arching of the eyebrow.
  • The head is thrown backward in bilateral cases (chin elevation) and tilted in unilateral cases.
