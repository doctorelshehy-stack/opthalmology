Retinal vein occlusion: pathogenesis & risk factors

Pathogenesis
  • Retinal artery and its corresponding vein share a common adventitial sheath so that arteriosclerotic changes of the artery compress the vein.
  • This leads to thrombus formation and venous obstruction with stagnation of blood flow.
  • Blood stagnation results in hypoxia, damage to endothelial cells and extravasation of blood constituents.
Classification of retinal vein occlusion: 1) Central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO). 2) Branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO).
Risk factors of retinal vein occlusion
  1. Old age.
  2. Systemic hypertension.
  3. Diabetes mellitus
  4. Abnormality of the blood; such as leukemia, sickle cell disease, polycythemia, dehydration and abnormalities in coagulation.
  5. Abnormality of the venous wall; such as phlebitis.
  6. Raised intraocular pressure.
