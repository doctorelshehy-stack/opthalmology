Clinical picture · signs

The classic triad

Mid-peripheral bone-spicule pigment, arteriolar attenuation and waxy disc pallor.

  1. Mid-peripheral pigment clumps appear as bone-spicules; they increase gradually in density and spread anteriorly and posteriorly.
  2. Arteriolar attenuation — narrowed retinal arterioles.
  3. Waxy disc pallor — a pale, waxy appearance of the optic disc.
The triad is the classic fundus signature. Pigment density and distribution progress over time.

Bone-spicules are mid-peripheral; the disc is waxy and the arterioles narrowed.
