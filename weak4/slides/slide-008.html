Retina · Vitreous

The vitreous

Structure
  • The vitreous is a clear gel occupying 2/3 of the globe.
  • It is formed of 99% water, hyaluronic acid, fine collagen network and few cells.
  • It is bounded:
    • Anteriorly by: lens, iris and ciliary body.
    • Posteriorly by: retina and optic disc.
Functions of the vitreous
  1. It stabilizes the volume of the globe, acts as a cushion of the retina.
  2. One of the optical media of the eye.
  3. It is firmly attached anteriorly to the peripheral retina, pars plana and around optic disc, and less firmly to the macula and retinal vessels.
Causes of vitreous hemorrhage
  1. Posterior vitreous detachment.
  2. Retinal breaks.
  3. Trauma.
  4. Central retinal vein occlusion.
  5. Proliferative retinopathies as proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR).
  6. Blood diseases as anemia and leukemia.
