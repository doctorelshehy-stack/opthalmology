Ectropion · paralytic

Facial nerve
palsy

Paralysis of the orbicularis affects the lower eyelid. The common cause is a lower motor neuron lesion of the facial nerve — Bell's palsy.

Cause and clinical picture

Due to paralysis of the orbicularis affecting the lower eyelid. The common cause is lower motor neuron lesions of the facial nerve (Bell's palsy).

Symptoms

  • Incomplete closure of the eyelids.
  • Lacrimation (failure of the lacrimal pump mechanism).

Signs

  • Incomplete blinking, lagophthalmos, inability to elevate the eyebrow.
  • Less defined nasolabial fold, and inability to pucker the mouth.

Temporary treatment

  • Help nerve regeneration: vitamin B complex, vasodilator, cortisone and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.
  • Help muscle: massage and electric stimulation.
  • Protect cornea: artificial tears during the day and ointment during sleep, as well as strapping of the lids.

If paralysis is permanent

  • Tarsorrhaphy: the upper and lower lid margins are stitched together to prevent exposure keratopathy.
  • Canthoplasty: lateral and medial, and shortening of the canthal tendon.
  • Subcutaneous fascia lata sling: fixed to the medial canthal tendon and passed subcutaneously below the lower lid to be fixed to the temporalis fascia.
  • Lateral tarsal strip: lateral part of tarsus fixed to the lateral canthal tendon.
  • Silicone or fascia lata sling encircling the eyelid.
