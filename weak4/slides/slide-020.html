Retina · Retinal vascular disorders

Hypertensive retinopathy

Systemic hypertension is diagnosed on blood pressure readings on several occasions of 140/90 or more.
Pathogenesis
  • Persistent mild hypertension leads to arteriosclerotic changes of blood vessels.
  • Elevated blood pressure leads to vasoconstriction with focal and generalized narrowing of retinal arterioles whose degree depends on the pre-existing involutional sclerosis.
  • Sustained hypertension leads to damage of vessel wall with breakdown of blood-retina barrier and vascular leakage.
  • Severe hypertension: leads to closure of retinal capillaries and ischemia.
  • Malignant hypertension, diastole more than 140, leads to damage of choroidal arterioles and occlusion of areas of choriocapillaris.
  • These changes lead to papilledema and exudative RD.
Symptoms
  1. May be asymptomatic.
  2. Blurred vision.
  3. Episodes of temporary visual loss.
Signs 1) Arteriolar narrowing
  • Focal and generalized narrowing of arterioles.
  • Severe hypertension may lead to obstruction of precapillary arterioles and the development of cotton wool spots.
