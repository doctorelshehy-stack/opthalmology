Retina · Retinal detachment

Rhegmatogenous RD: clinical picture

Symptoms
  • Flashes of light (photopsia):
    • It is caused by traction at the sites of vitreoretinal adhesions.
    • Traction leads to either separation of vitreoretinal adhesion or tearing away a piece of the underling retina.
  • Floaters (Musca volitans): are moving vitreous opacities perceived by the patient.
  • The patient complains of progressive black curtain as a result of the development of progressive field defect caused by spread of subretinal fluid.
Signs
  • Diminution of V/A depends on extension of RD. Marked loss of visual acuity occurs if the macula is detached by subretinal fluid (Macula off RD).
  • The intraocular pressure is lower than normal eye.
  • The red reflex becomes grey.
  • Mild iritis and pigment cells in retrolental vitreous by slit lamp examination.
  • Fundus picture:
    • The detached retina is convex and grey.
    • The retina is folded and undulates with eye movement.
    • Retinal break in the retina appears reddish pink.
