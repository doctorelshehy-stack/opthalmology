Definition and fate

Blood in the anterior chamber. It collects at the bottom of the A.C. and acquires an upper horizontal level.

Usually, blood becomes absorbed within 7 days via the iris crypts and the angle of the A.C.

Complications

  1. Recurrence (rebleeding).
  2. Secondary open glaucoma — haemorrhagic glaucoma — or secondary angle-closure glaucoma due to organisation of blood at the angle, leading to peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS).
  3. Chemical irritative iridocyclitis.
  4. Blood staining of the cornea, with increased IOP.

Blood settles inferiorly; the upper border forms a horizontal fluid level.
