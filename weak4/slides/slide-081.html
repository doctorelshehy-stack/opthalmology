Pupil · Light reflex Week 4 · Ophthalmology

Reflex pathway

Light on one eye,
both pupils constrict

The afferent limb travels in the optic nerve and tract to the pretectal nucleus; the efferent limb travels in the oculomotor nerve to the ciliary ganglion and sphincter.

  • Stimulus: light thrown on one eye.
  • Receptors: rods and cones.
  • Afferent: axons of retinal ganglion cells in the optic nerve → optic tract, leaving it before the LGB to enter the midbrain and relay in the pretectal nucleus. An intercalated neuron projects from the pretectal nucleus to the Edinger–Westphal nucleus on both sides.
  • Centre: Edinger–Westphal (parasympathetic) nucleus on both sides.
  • Efferent: parasympathetic preganglionic fibres within the oculomotor nerve → relay in the ciliary ganglion → postganglionic fibres within short ciliary nerves to the sphincter pupillae.
  • Response: constriction of the ipsilateral pupil (direct reflex) and of the contralateral pupil (consensual reflex).

Afferent: optic nerve/tract → pretectal nucleus → E–W. Efferent: CN III → ciliary ganglion → sphincter.
