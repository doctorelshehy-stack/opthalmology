Retina · Artery occlusion

Central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO): early picture

Causes
  • Atherosclerosis related thrombus at the level of lamina cribrosa is the most common cause of central retinal artery occlusion.
  • It may also be caused by calcific emboli.
Symptoms
  • Acute painless loss of vision except when cilioretinal artery preserves central vision.
  • Fibrin-platelet emboli pass through retinal circulation cause amaurosis fugax.
Signs 1) Early CRAO
  • Profound or total (amaurotic pupil) afferent pupillary defect.
  • Attenuated arteries and veins with segmentation of blood column (cattle trucking).
  • Swollen and white retina (due to ischemia and coagulative necrosis).
  • In eyes with cilioretinal artery part of the macula will remain of normal color.
  • Fovea is red (cherry red spot).
