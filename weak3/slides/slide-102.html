Strabismus · Concomitant Squint

Types of Concomitant Squint

According to the direction of deviation
  • 1) Convergent If the visual axes are convergent (esotropia).
  • 2) Divergent If the visual axes are divergent (exotropia).
  • 3) Vertical Downward (hypotropia) or upward (hypertropia).
  • 4) Mixed If there is combined vertical and horizontal deviations.
According to the laterality of deviation
  • 1) Unilateral (uniocular) If the deviation is always manifested by the squinting eye.
  • 2) Alternating If the deviation is manifested by either eye at different times (either eye is squinting at a time).
According to constancy of deviation: Exotropia may be
  • 1) Constant (permanent)
    • Deviation is present all the time.
    • There is normal or low AC/A ratio.
  • 2) Intermittent (periodic)
    • The deviation is not constantly present all the time.
    • Like heterophoria, there is normal fusional amplitude, no amblyopia, and bifixation.
