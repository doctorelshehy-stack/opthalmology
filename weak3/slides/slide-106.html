Strabismus · Cover Test

3) Results: Manifest Squint in Concomitant Squint

1) In manifest squint: Covering the straight eye results in movement of the deviated eye to take up fixation.
1) In concomitant squint

The movement of the covered and uncovered eyes are equal (i.e. the primary angle of deviation equals the secondary angle of deviation).

Covering one eye then removing the cover after a while so that both eyes are uncovered (and then the same is done for the other eye) and observe:

N.B.: On covering the fixing eye in unilateral squint with eccentric (extrafoveal) fixation, the deviating eye fixes the object without movement from its position (i.e. remains deviating and so the corneal reflex is not at the center of the pupil).
(a) Unilateral or alternating

In unilateral squint with centric (foveal) fixation: One eye is always fixing (with no movement) and the other eye fixes when the other eye is covered and when the cover is removed it deviates again.

In alternating squint: Either eye moves to take up fixation when the other eye is covered.

(b) Convergent or divergent

In convergent squint: The uncovered eye moves outward away from the midline to take up fixation.

In divergent squint: The uncovered eye moves inwards towards the midline to take up fixation.
