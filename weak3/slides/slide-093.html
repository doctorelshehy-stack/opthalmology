Strabismus · Paralytic Squint

Explanation: Hering Law of Equal Innervation

Normally: The brain sends nerve impulses to both eyes for fixation in any direction, which is equally distributed between both eyes (Herring law).

When the paralyzed eye is fixing (the normal eye is covered):

a
More impulses to the paralyzed muscleThe brain sends more nerve impulses to the paralyzed muscle to force it to contract.
b
Equal impulses to the contralateral synergistAt the same time, equal amount of these nerve impulses are sent to the synergistic muscle of the other normal eye (contralateral synergist) and so produce overaction of that muscle (Hering law of equal innervation).
c
Increased movement of the normal eye under the coverThis leads to increased movement of the normal eye under the cover with increased angle of secondary deviation.
