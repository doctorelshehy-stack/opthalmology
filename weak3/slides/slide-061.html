Uveitis · Iridocyclitis

Signs 5-10: Iris, Pupil, Ciliary Body, Lens, Vitreous, Tension

5) Iris & 6) Pupil

  • 5) Iris — a) Loss of iris pattern: Due iris exudates.
  • b) Muddy iris: Due to altered (yellowish-brown) color and loss of iris pattern.
  • 6) Pupil — a) Miosis: Due to engorged iris vessels and spasm of sphincter pupillae.
  • b) Irregular: Due to posterior synechiae.
  • c) Sluggish reaction on light stimulation: Due to iris exudates.
  • 7) Ciliary body:
    • Tenderness.
    • Transient myopia (due to spasm of ciliary muscle).

8) Lens · 9) Vitreous · 10) Tension

  • 8) Lens: Lenticular precipitates are seen after pupil dilatation with rupture of posterior synechiae.
  • 9) Vitreous: Vitreous turbidity (cloudiness) due to exudates.
  • 10) Tension: May be high due to angle block by plasmoid aqueous and exudates.
Miosis Irregular pupil Sluggish reaction

Posterior synechiae make the pupil irregular; iris exudates make its reaction sluggish; ciliary spasm gives transient myopia.
