Strabismus · Paralytic Squint

Signs: Primary and Secondary Deviation

1) Ocular deviation and angles of deviation (Fig. 11-7)

a) Primary deviation (When the normal eye is fixating):

  • Ocular deviation occurs in a direction opposite to that of the action of the paralyzed muscle (e.g. inwards in right LR paralysis).
  • The paralyzed ms. loses its tone so, the antagonistic draws the eye toward it).

b) Secondary deviation (When the paralyzed eye is fixating):

  • Is the deviation of the normal eye when it is covered by a screen to force the patient to fix with the paralyzed eye.
c) The angle of secondary deviation is much larger than the angle of primary deviation: Due to over action of the contralateral synergist of the paralyzed muscle (i.e. synergistic muscle of the other normal eye).
