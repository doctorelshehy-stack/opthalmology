Glaucoma · Acquired Glaucoma — Secondary Type

Local Causes (2): A.C. & Iris / C.B. Disorders

2) Anterior chamber disorders

Open angle:

  • Any increase in the viscosity of the aqueous by abnormal inflammatory cells, bloods, exudates or malignant cells would block the pores of the trabecular mesh work leading to 2ry open angle glaucoma.
  • As in hypopyon and hyphema.

Closed angle: anterior dislocation of the lens → pupillary block.

3) Iris and C.B. disorders — Iridocyclitis

Open angle:

  • Acute iritis causes edema of the trabecular meshwork which may obstruct the aqueous out flow causing rise of IOP.

Closed angle:

  • In subacute and chronic iridocyclitis: permanent PAS developed and sometimes ring pupillary posterior synechia causing marked rise of IOP.
Open angle: blockage of the pores of the trabecular mesh work or oedema of the trabecular meshwork. Closed angle: the angle is obliterated by anterior lens dislocation, ring pupillary posterior synechia or PAS.
