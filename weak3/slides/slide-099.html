Strabismus · Ocular Motor Palsies

Ophthalmoplegia & Secondary Changes

2) Ophthalmoplegia (ocular muscle palsies)
Total

Paralysis of all extraocular and intraocular muscles.

External

Paralysis of all extraocular muscles.

Internal

Paralysis of all intraocular muscles.

Complications: secondary changes to ocular motor palsies
1) Extraocular muscles (Motor sequelae)
  • a) Overaction (contracture) of the ipsilateral antagonist.
  • b) Overaction of the contralateral synergist (excessive impulses).
  • c) Underaction of the contralateral antagonist (inhibitory impulses) [ 2ry palsy].
2) Vision (sensory sequelae)

Suppression or even strabismic amblyopia may occur in longstanding cases in young patients.
