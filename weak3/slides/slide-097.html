Strabismus · Paralytic Squint

5) Ocular Torticollis (Compensatory Head Posture)

Direction

Occurs in the direction of action of the paralyzed muscle.

  • In horizontal deviation → simple (face turn).
  • In vertical deviation → compound: (face turn, chin elevation or depression and head tilt).
Example

Face turn to the right side in right LR palsy (to avoid looking to the right side as diplopia is maximum at that side).

Differentiation: Ocular torticollis must be differentiated from congenital torticollis (birth trauma) where the sternomastoid muscle is fibrosed in one side of the neck.
Rule of direction: All signs and symptoms of paralytic squint occur in the direction of action of the paralyzed muscle except the ocular deviation (squint itself) which occurs on the opposite side.
CHP: The compensatory head posture is adopted by the patient to avoid diplopia and to maintain binocular single vision (common in congenital cases).
