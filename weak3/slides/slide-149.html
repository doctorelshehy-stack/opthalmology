Retina · Retinal detachment

II. Tractional retinal detachment

The sensory retina is pulled off by contracting fibrous tissue on the retina surface.

Causes
  • Proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR).
  • Sickle cell retinopathy.
  • Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP).
  • Penetrating posterior segment trauma.
Symptoms
  • Slowly progressive loss of vision and visual field defects.
Signs
  1. No retinal breaks.
  2. Fibrous membranes on the surface of the retina.
  3. The surface of the retina takes concave appearance.
  4. Restricted mobility of the retina.
  5. No shift of subretinal fluid.
Complications
  1. Visual loss if traction involves the macula.
  2. Traction may result in a retinal tear. The RD progresses rapidly and leads to combined tractional and rhegmatogenous RD.
Treatment
  • Pars plana vitrectomy with membrane removal.
