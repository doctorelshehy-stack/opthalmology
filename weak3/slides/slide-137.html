Retina · Vein occlusion

Central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO)

Symptom: sudden unilateral diminution of vision often noticed in the early morning. Central retinal vein occlusion may be non-ischemic or ischemic.

Sign Non-ischemic (Fig.12-8) Ischemic (Fig.12-9)
Vision Moderate diminution of vision > 6/60. Severe diminution of vision < 6/60.
Afferent pupillary defect Absent or mild afferent pupillary defect. Marked afferent pupillary defect.
Retinal veins Mild tortuous and dilated all branches of central retinal vein. Marked tortuous, engorged all branches of central retinal vein.
Hemorrhages Dot-blot and flame-shaped hemorrhages more numerous in the periphery. Extensive dot-blot and flame shaped hemorrhages in the periphery and center.
Cotton wool spots Few or absent cotton wool spots. Numerous cotton wool spots.
Disc edema Mild to moderate disc edema. Severe disc edema.
Macular edema Mild macular edema and hemorrhage. Marked macular edema and hemorrhage.
Fluorescein angiography Shows good capillary perfusion. Shows extensive areas of capillary non-perfusion.
Prognosis 50% resolves and V/A near normal. 50% of cases develop neovascularization.
