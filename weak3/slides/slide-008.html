Glaucoma · Intraocular Pressure

Measurement of IOP: Tonometry

Indentation · Applanation · Air puff

1) Indentation

Schiotz tonometer

  • While the patient is supine the tonometer is placed perpendicular to measure the indentation of the cornea produced by a known weight.

Disadvantage: false low readings when scleral rigidity is low as in: high myopia and previous ocular surgery.

2) Applanation

Goldmann tonometer

  • On the slit lamp, the IOP is measured by the force needed by a cone to flatten fixed small area of the cornea (3.06mm).

More accurate as it is not affected by scleral rigidity.

3) Air puff tonometer

Non-contact tonometer

  • Non-contact tonometer.
Applanation (Goldmann) is more accurate because it is not affected by scleral rigidity, whereas indentation (Schiotz) gives false low readings when scleral rigidity is low (high myopia, previous ocular surgery).
