Glaucoma · Primary Infantile Glaucoma

Clinical Picture: Signs (1–5)

Cornea · Eyeball · Sclera · A.C. · Iris

1) Cornea — 3 signs
  • a) Corneal edema.
  • b) Corneal enlargement due to rise of IOP, more than 12mm in diameter.
  • c) Corneal opacity due to tears in descemet's membrane (Haab's striae).
Signs 2 to 5
  • 2) Eyeball: enlarged eyeball due to stretching of its outer coat as a result of increased IOP.
  • 3) Sclera: bluish due to globe enlargement and thin sclera.
  • 4) Anterior chamber: deep due to flattening & posterior displacement of the lens.
  • 5) Iris: tremulous, atrophic, lightly pigmented and the pupil later get larger and sluggish.
