b) Concomitant Squint (Heterotropia): Definition & Etiology

Definition

Ocular deviation with abnormal direction of the visual axes of both eyes relative to each other in which the angle of deviation is constant in different directions of gaze.

Etiology

Any obstacle (sensory, motor or central cause) which interferes with the development of binocular vision during the first 6 years of life (period of flux):

  • 2) Motor obstacles: Weakness of the extraocular muscles.
  • 3) Central obstacles: Due to defective fusion center in the brain.
1) Sensory obstacles
A) Error of refraction
  • 1) Hypermetropia (more than 3D): Excessive accommodation (to see objects clearly) → Stimulate convergence → Convergent Squint.
  • 2) Myopia: Lack or weak accommodation (as it is rarely used) → Less convergence → Divergent Squint.
B) Defective vision in one eye (Stimulus deprivation amblyopia)
  • 1) Complete congenital ptosis.
  • 2) Corneal opacities.
  • 3) Lens opacities.
