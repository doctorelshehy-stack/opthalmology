Uveitis · Iridocyclitis

Sign 4: Anterior Chamber

  • a) Deep: Due to plasmoid aqueous.
  • b) Aqueous flare: The light path is visible through the hazy (turbid) aqueous humor on slit-lamp examination.
  • c) Hypopyon: Sterile pus in the anterior chamber.

Sign 4d

Hyphema

Rarely occurs if the iris vessels are engorged in hemorrhagic iritis due to:

  • Diabetes (Rubeosis Iridis).
  • Trauma, Gout.
  • Herpes, Tuberculosis or Gonorrhea.

Aqueous flare = the light path visible through the hazy (turbid) aqueous humor on slit-lamp examination · Hypopyon = sterile pus in the anterior chamber.
