Strabismus · Concomitant Squint

Suppression in Squint

Definition

Active inhibition of vision in one eye.

Principle
  1. With the onset of squint, the 2 retinal images of any object fall on 2 noncorresponding points of the 2 retinae.
  2. To avoid diplopia, the brain neglects the blurred image seen by the squinting eye.
Types of fixation
  • a) Centric (foveal) fixation: By the macula.
  • b) Eccentric (extrafoveal) fixation: Occurs in unilateral squint in which an eccentric part of the retina takes the function of the macula which is markedly suppressed i.e. a false macula.
N.B.: Amblyopia ex-anopsia is the old term which was used to denote strabismic amblyopia.
