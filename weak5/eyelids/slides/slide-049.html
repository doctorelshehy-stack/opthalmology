Contraindications

When not to
operate

Four situations in which correcting the ptosis first may harm the patient — most importantly, recent complete third nerve palsy with an uncorrected squint.

01

Recent complete 3rd nerve palsy

The patient has a paralytic squint without diplopia, because the ptosed lid covers the squinting eye. If the ptosis is corrected before the squint, diplopia will occur — so correct the squint first.

02

Neuroparalytic keratitis or absent Bell's phenomenon

Avoid surgery in the presence of neuroparalytic keratitis or an absent Bell's phenomenon.

03

Myasthenic ptosis

Myasthenic ptosis is medical, not surgical.

04

Corneal anaesthesia

Avoid exposure keratitis — the ptosis protects the cornea.
