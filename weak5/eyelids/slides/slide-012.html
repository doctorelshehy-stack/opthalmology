Blood, lymph and nerves

A rich supply,
a surgical map

The lid has a dual arterial arcade, predictable venous and lymphatic drainage, and motor nerves that explain the signs of facial, oculomotor and sympathetic palsy.

Arterial supply

  • Medial palpebral branch of the ophthalmic artery.
  • Lateral palpebral branch of the lacrimal artery.
  • Vessels pass anterior to the tarsus and divide into a marginal arcade near the lid margin and a peripheral arcade near the upper border of tarsus in the upper lid.
  • In the lower lid there is only one arch near the free edge.

Venous drainage

  • Pretarsal plexus → temporal and facial veins.
  • Post-tarsal plexus → ophthalmic veins.

Lymphatic drainage

  • Lateral part → preauricular and parotid lymph nodes.
  • Medial part → submandibular lymph nodes.
  • Finally → upper deep cervical lymph nodes.
Nerve supply — source study prompt: describe the nerve supply of the eyelid. Motor supply includes orbicularis (facial), levator (oculomotor) and Müller's muscle (sympathetic).

Upper lid: marginal and peripheral arcades. Lower lid: one arch near the free edge.
