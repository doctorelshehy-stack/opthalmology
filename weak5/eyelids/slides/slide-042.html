Lid position · Lagophthalmos Eyelids · Ophthalmology

Etiology

General, local,
lid and globe

Causes range from unconsciousness and general anaesthesia to lid malposition, proptosis and facial palsy. The cornea is the structure at risk.

General causes

Occurs in extreme debility, unconsciousness and general anaesthesia.

Local causes

Physiological: occurs during sleep (especially in children) due to lack of tone of orbicularis.

Pathological — lesions in the lid

  1. Severe ectropion.
  2. Structural loss (coloboma).
  3. Postoperative (overcorrection of ptosis or entropion).
  4. Spastic lid retraction, e.g. thyroid ophthalmopathy.
  5. Lower motor neuron facial palsy.

Lesions in the globe

Proptosis, anterior staphyloma, buphthalmos, etc.

Complications

  • Cornea: exposure keratitis develops, which may end in corneal ulceration, perforation and loss of the eye.
  • Conjunctiva: may be exposed, dry and xerotic.
The severity of the cause determines the urgency of corneal protection.
