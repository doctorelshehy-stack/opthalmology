Refraction · Presbyopia

Presbyopia

Physiological recession of the near point of the eye to the degree that makes near vision uncomfortable or even difficult.

  • It occurs about the age of 45 years in an emmetropic person.
  • While earlier in hypermetropes.
  • And later in myopes.

Symptoms

  1. Difficulty in reading & near work.
    • Patient needs to hold the book at a greater distance from the eye.
  2. Accommodative asthenopia.

Treatment

Addition of a biconvex lens to the distant prescription in the following manner:

  1. Correct errors of refraction for far.
  2. Measure the near point (Punctum Proximum), while the patient is wearing his far correction.
  3. Calculate the amplitude of accommodation.
    • If the near point is 50 cm, e.g. the amplitude is 100/50 (= 2D).
  4. Keep 1/3 of the amplitude of accommodation in reserve to ensure comfort.
  5. Allow the patient to use the remaining two thirds of accommodation and add plus lenses to make the sum 3 dioptres; because the standard working distance is 33 cm (100/33 = 30).

Example

A presbyope sees 6/6 with + 1 D.

  • The near point with far glasses is 50 cm.
  • The amplitude of accommodation = 1 / F =100/50(2D).
  • The reserved accommodation: 1/3 X 2 = 2/3 D (= 0.7D).
  • The allowed accommodation: 2 - 0.7 = 1.3 D.

The standard reading distance is 33 cm.

  • The refractive power needed for comfortable reading = 1/0.33 = 3 D.
  • Therefore, the lens required to correct presbyopia = 3 -1.3 = 1.7 D.
  • This should be added for his far correction i.e. reading glasses will be: 1.7 D + 1 D (far correction) = 2.7 D.
