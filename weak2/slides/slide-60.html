Conjunctiva · Trachoma — corneal manifestations

Pannus types and trachomatous corneal ulcer

Clinical types of pannus

  • Thin pannus (pannus tenius): mild, commonest.
  • Vascular pannus (pannus vasculosus): vessels much, cells scanty.
  • Fleshy pannus (pannus carnosus): cells much, vessels few.
  • Annular pannus (pannus annulosus): all around the cornea.
  • Cicatrized pannus (pannus ciccus): healed pannus formed of crescentic whitish scar of opacity in the upper cornea with serrated edge (due to Herbert's pits). This opacity is called nebula ex-pannus.

Course of pannus

  • Progressive pannus: cellular infiltration precedes beyond the corneal vascularization.
  • Regressive pannus: the vascularization extends a short distance beyond the cellular infiltration; this is due to later regress of the cellular infiltration.

2) Trachomatous corneal ulcer

  • Usually superficial, situated at the lower border of the pannus.
  • Its shape is linear with a horizontal long axis.
  • Corneal ulceration may also occur due to mechanical corneal injury by maldirected lashes or PTCs.
