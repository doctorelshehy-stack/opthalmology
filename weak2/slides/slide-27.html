Cornea · Herpes zoster ophthalmicus

Signs: the ophthalmic division of the Trigeminal

Unilateral and the ocular manifestations depend on the affected branch:

1) The ophthalmic division of the Trigeminal:

  • a) The frontal branch is the most commonly affected.
    • Clinically (Lid affection): neuralgic pain and an area of erythema followed by the appearance of vesicles: grouped unilateral and follow the distribution of a nerve.
  • b) The lacrimal branch is rarely affected.
  • c) The naso-ciliary branch which will lead to
    • Corneal affection:
      • a- Punctate epithelial keratitis with positive stain by rose Bengal only.
      • b- Vesicles may appear in the epithelium and these may ulcerate, the ulcers are never dendritic but show positive fluorescein test, and loss of luster.
      • c- Subepithelial keratitis and disciform keratitis may occur.
      • d- Corneal hypothesia may occur with the development of neurotropic keratitis.
    • Scleritis.
    • Iridocyclitis with the occurrence of secondary glaucoma.
    • Skin of the medial canthus & the tip of the nose show the typical vesicles on the erythematous base.
