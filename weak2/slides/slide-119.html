Anisometropia

Refraction of both eyes shows considerable difference (more than 4 D)

Etiology

  • Developmental: Common
  • Acquired: Unilateral aphakia, After some ocular surgeries.

Types

  1. Simple anisometropia: One eye is emmetropic and the other eye is ametropic (myopic or hypermetropic).
  2. Compound anisometropia: Both eyes are hypermetropic or myopic.
  3. Mixed anisometropia: One eye is myopic & the other is hypermetropic.

Clinical Picture

  1. Asthenopia.
  2. Loss of binocular vision: The vision will be:
    • Alternating: If one eye is myopic (used for near vision) and the other eye is emmetropic or moderately hypermetropic (used for far vision).
    • Uniocular: If the highly ametropic eye becomes amblyope (anisometropic amblyopia).

Treatment

  1. Contact lenses
  2. Secondary IOL implantation (in unilateral aphakia)
  3. Refractive surgery
  4. Aniseikonic lenses: A glass plate which causes magnification without introducing any appreciable refractive power.
