Conjunctiva · Bacterial conjunctivitis

P.C: complications and treatment

Complications

  • Corneal ulcers: They are common because:
    • Gonococci can invade an intact corneal epithelium.
    • The conjunctival edema:
      • Presses on the limbal capillaries, thus impairing corneal nutrition
      • Forms a gutter around the cornea, which helps accumulation of organisms and toxins.
    • The proteolytic enzymes from leucocytes and gonococcal exotoxin

    Types:

    • Marginal: The most common and may form a ring ulcer (coalescence of multiple ulcers).
    • Central and paracentral: Rare, but commonly perforate.
  • Spastic ectropion: May occur.
  • Iridocyclitis: Usually caused by diffusion of toxins.

Treatment

  • Prophylaxis: as MPC.
  • Curative: As MPC but more energetic & more frequent.
    • Systemic: broad spectrum antibiotics.
    • Local: Broad spectrum antibiotics eye drops and ointment.
    • Mydriatic – Cycloplegic drops in corneal involvement.
