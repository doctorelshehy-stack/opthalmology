Refraction · Aphakia

Aphakia

Definition

Aphakia means absence of the crystalline lens from the eye.

Etiology

  • Congenital: Very rare.
  • Acquired:
    1. a) Post-traumatic: The lens matter may be absorbed (mainly in children) after capsular rupture.
    2. b) Post-operative: After cataract surgery or clear lens extraction.

Optical Problems

  1. Hypermetropia.
  2. Loss of accommodation.
  3. Astigmatism:
    • Against the rule astigmatism may occur after cataract extraction due to contraction of the wound scar with flattening of vertical meridian of cornea.
    • Its amount varies according to the used procedure.
  4. Anisometropia: In unilateral aphakia; due to marked difference in the refractive power of both eyes

Signs

  1. A scar for lens extraction may be present.
  2. Absence of two of the three Purkinje - Sanson images.
  3. Deep A.C - tremulous iris (iridodenesis).
  4. Pupil:
    • May be round or distorted (Previous trauma or surgery).
    • The pupillary area is jet black in color.
    • Fine strands of after cataract may be seen.
  5. Ultrasonography: Confirms absence of the lens.

Treatment

  1. Bilateral aphakia: Two pairs of glasses (one for distance and one for near).
  2. Unilateral aphakia: IOL or contact lenses.
N.B.Glasses can't be prescribed for unilateral aphakia as the aphakic eye will need large plus lens which will magnify its retinal image by 30 % resulting in anisokonia with difficult fusion and binocular diplopia.
