Cataract · Senile Cortical

4) Hypermature Stage and the Morgagnian Form

4) Hypermature stage of senile cortical cataract:

Symptoms: Marked painless drop of vision.

Signs

  • V.A: marked drop of vision (hand movement).
  • Slit lamp examination through a fully dilated pupil will show “Signs of Hypermaturity”:
    • a) Total or complete grayish white opacification of the lens cortex.
    • b) Calcium & cholesterol crystals deposits on the lens capsule.
  • Escape of the liquefied cortical material through the capsule will lead to shrinkage of the lens as evidenced by:
    • Reappearance of the iris shadow.
    • Deep anterior chamber.
    • Tremulous iris “Iridodenesis” due to lack of iris support.
  • Extension of the degenerative process to the zonules will lead:
    • Partial rupture of zonules with subluxation of the lens.
    • Complete rupture of zonules with lens dislocation.

Morgagnian form of Hypermature Senile Cortical Cataract

Pathology:

  • Impaired permeability of the lens capsule will lead to retention of the liquefied degenerated cortical material and absorption of water from the aqueous with lens intumescence.
  • The nucleus which resists proteolysis becomes freely mobile in the liquefied cortex.
