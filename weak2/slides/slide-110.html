Hypermetropia: accommodation, clinical picture & complications

Role of accommodation in hypermetropia

Accommodation increases the refractive power of the lens and consequently the eye; hence hypermetropia is the only error of refraction that can be corrected by accommodation (Fig. 8-13).

Symptoms

  • In children: No symptoms because accommodation is strong and corrects all hypermetropia.
  • As age advances:
    • Diminution of vision.
    • Accommodative asthenopia: First for near and later for far and near.
    • Early presbyopia: The patient has to accommodate for far and near resulting in exhaustion of accommodation.

Signs: Only with high errors

  • The globe is small with small cornea & small pupil.
  • The anterior chamber is shallow & the angle is narrow.
  • Fundus shows:
    • Bright vascular reflex (water - silk appearance).
    • Tortuous retinal vessels.
    • Pseudo-papilledema: Blurred disc margin due to small lamina cribrosa with crowded nerve fibers. (D.D. papilledema and papillitis).

Complications

  1. Squint: Apparent divergent - true convergent.
  2. Primary angle closure glaucoma may occur in marked hypermetropia.
