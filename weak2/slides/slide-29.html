Cornea · Fungal ulcer

Yeast and filamentous fungi

  • Yeast fungi (candida).
  • Filamentous fungi: Fusarium, Aspergillus.

1) Candida ulcers

Occurs in immunocompromised eyes:

  • Prolonged use of steroids.
  • Dry eye.
  • Exposure keratitis.
  • Herpes simplex keratitis.

Clinical picture:

  • Shows a distinct oval outline with a plaque-like surface & stromal infiltration with dispersed satellite lesions.
  • Candida ulcer shows dense suppuration similar to bacterial keratitis.

2) Filamentous Fungal Ulcers

Cause:

  • They infect normal cornea following mild trauma with vegetables matter.

Clinical picture:

  • Characteristic grey or dirty white dry textured surface with elevated margins.
  • Featherly extensions beneath the epithelium into the adjacent stroma.
  • Satellite lesions may occur (due to micro abscesses in the surrounding tissues).
  • If the infection is deep, endothelial plaque may be seen.
  • Anterior chamber reaction with hypopyon is often present.
