Cornea · Dendritic ulcer

Definition and etiology

Definition: It's a primary infective corneal ulcer. Characterized by being linear, superficial, crenated or dendritic in shape.

Etiology:

Causative organism: Herpes simplex virus:

  • Which is an epitheliotropic typical virus.
  • Herpes simplex infection gives no immunity (epitheliotropic).

Source of infection:

  • Primary infection: occurs in children.
    • It may be subclinical or manifests as severe follicular conjunctivitis with fever, LN enlargement & may affect the cornea.
  • Recurrence:
    • After the primary infection, the virus becomes dormant in the mucocutaneous junctions, the patient becomes a carrier.
    • The virus can be activated by heat with the formation of vesicles around the genitals, mouth, lids & the cornea.

Predisposing factors:

  • The virus is activated by heat, so may be also called herpes fibrillis:
    • It may follow fevers as common cold, influenza, pneumonia, typhoid.
    • It may also follow the use of immunosuppressives as local steroids.
