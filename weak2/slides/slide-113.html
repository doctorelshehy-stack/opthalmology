Types of astigmatism & the rule of regular astigmatism

Types of corneal astigmatism

  • Regular:
    • The meridians of the highest and lowest power (the principal meridians) are at right angle to each other.
    • Also, there is a gradual and regular change from the meridian of the highest to that of the lowest power.
  • Irregular:
    • The meridians of maximal and minimal powers are not at right angle to each other.
    • the change from the highest to the lowest powers is not gradual or regular.
    • It can't be corrected by ordinary glasses.
    • It occurs in keratoconus and corneal scars.

Types of Regular Astigmatism

  • Straight astigmatism: The principal meridians are at 90 and 180 degrees.
  • Oblique astigmatism: The principal meridians are perpendicular to each other but not at 90 and 180 degrees.

The rule of Regular Astigmatism

  • The vertical meridian is normally more curved (more powerful) than the horizontal.
  • According to this rule, regular astigmatism can be classified into:
With the ruleAgainst the rule
The vertical meridian is more curved.The vertical meridian is less curved.
Less hypermetropic or more myopic.More hypermetropic or less myopic.
N.B.Physiological astigmatism: Some degree of astigmatism is normally present where the vertical meridian is slightly more curved than the horizontal (about 0.25 D) presumably due to pressure of the upper eyelid on the cornea.
