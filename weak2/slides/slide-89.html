Cataract · Senile Cataract

2) Senile Nuclear Cataract

Definition

It is an acquired nuclear opacity, occurring in senile patient after the age of 50 years, in the absence of any local or systemic disease.

Features

  • Bilateral, progressive hard cataract.

Pathology

  • It is the exaggerated dehydration of the lens nucleus with the subsequent denaturation and opacification of the lens nucleus proteins.

Clinically

Symptoms: Painless progressive diminution of vision due to:

  • Progressive index myopia: Due to the increased RI of the nucleus = this can lead to what we call second sight (correct the presbyopia).
  • Progressive opacification of the lens nucleus will lead to progressive diminution of vision, but mainly by day due to the central location of the opacity = day blindness.

Signs

  • 1) V.A: progressive failure and finally H.M., according to the stage.
  • 2) Slit lamp through a fully dilated pupil: progressive lens opacification.
  • 3) Iris shadow is at first positive but latter on when the cataract becomes mature, it turns negative (rare).
