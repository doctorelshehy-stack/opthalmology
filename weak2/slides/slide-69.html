Conjunctiva · Phlyctenular kerato-conjunctivitis

Phlyctenular: signs, differential diagnosis and treatment

Signs:

A. Conjunctival manifestations: (Simple phlyctenular conjunctivitis)

  • Site: it occurs in any part of conjunctiva or cornea but commonly at limbus and bulbar conjunctiva.
  • Shape: The phlycten: is round nodule, size (1-3 mm), greyish, elevated, surrounded by small area of conjunctival injection.

B. Corneal manifestations

  • Corneal phlycten: usually limbal.
  • Phlyctenular ulcer:
    • Marginal ulcer: single (limbal).
    • Ring ulcer: due to fusion of multiple limbal ulcers.
  • Phlyctenular pannus: cellular infiltration associated with vascularization of the superficial layers of the cornea.

Differential diagnosis: Phlyctens must be differentiated from:

  • Pinguecula
  • Nodules of episcleritis
  • Limbal spring catarrh
  • Phlyctenular pannus must be differentiated from trachomatous pannus.

Treatment

  • General: Treatment of the cause.
  • Local:
    • Topical steroids give dramatic effect (drops and ointment).
    • Antibiotic for secondary infection.
