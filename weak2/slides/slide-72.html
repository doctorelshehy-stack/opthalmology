Pterygium: treatment and operations

Treatment

  • No treatment if the pterygium is small and stationary, because it has a high incidence of recurrence.
  • Indications for surgery:
    • Progressive pterygium.
    • Interfere with vision (encroaches on papillary area).
    • Cosmetic.
    • Recurrent pterygium with diplopia and limitation of ocular movement.

The operations

  • Simple excision: with the edges of conjunctiva are sutured without any raw area. It has a high degree of recurrence.
  • Excision with bare sclera: The pterygium is excised leaving a raw area of sclera allowing the corneal epithelium to cover the bared cornea before the conjunctival epithelium.

To prevent recurrence

  • Covered by a conjunctival, amniotic membrane or corneal graft.
  • Exposed to postoperative Beta irradiation (Strontium 90).
  • Exposed to Mitomycin C, either:
    • Preoperative intralesional injection,
    • Intraoperative topical application.
    • Or postoperative eyedrops
