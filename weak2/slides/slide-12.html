Hypopyon ulcer (Acute serpiginous ulcer)

Definition: It's a primary infective corneal ulcer characterized by the presence of severe toxic iridocyclitis and the early formation of hypopyon.

Cause: It is usually caused by pyogenic organisms.

Causative organisms:

  • Pneumococcus is the commonest and is responsible for formation of typical hypopyon ulcer (80% of cases).
  • Morax-axenfeld diplobacilli, staph, strept, pseudomonas aerginosa (due to contaminated fluorescein dye).
  • Viral as herpes and fungal as candida, can also form ulcers with hypopyon, they are now becoming more common due to the prolonged abuse of local antibiotics and steroids.

Source of infection:

  • Chronic pneumococcal dacryocystitis in pneumococcal cases.
  • Angular blepharo-conjunctivitis in Morax- axenfeld cases.
  • Contaminated solutions of fluorescein in pseudomonas cases.
  • Scratch by dirty fingers, contaminated corneal F.B.
