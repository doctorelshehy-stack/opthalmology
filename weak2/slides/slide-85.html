Cataract · Senile Cortical

1) Senile Cortical Cataract — Pathogenesis and Incipient Stage

Pathogenesis

Two pathological changes occur in senile cortical cataract:

  • Hydration, with the appearance of water droplets between the lens fibers.
  • Denaturation and coagulation of the lens proteins leading to opacification of the lens.
Clinical picture

The progression goes through 4 stages:

1) Incipient stage of senile cortical cataract:

Symptoms:

  • Painless gradual progressive diminution of vision.
  • Uniocular diplopia: due to the irregular refraction
  • Haloes around the light due to the prismatic effect of water droplets between the lens fibers.

Signs:

  • V.A: is not markedly affected.
  • Slit lamp examination through a fully dilated pupil will show peripheral opacities:
    • Shape: triangular or wedge shaped (the apex towards the pupil).
    • Color: grayish white opacities.
    • Progress: they increase in number, progress both axially to the center of the lens and superficially affecting further layers of the cortex.
