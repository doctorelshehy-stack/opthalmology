Conjunctiva · Trachoma

Complications and treatment of trachoma

1) Eyelid complications

  • Trichiasis.
  • Entropion. It more commonly affects the upper lid.
  • Ptosis: is partly due to infiltration of Muller's muscle and partly mechanical due to heaviness of the upper lid by severe infiltration (large follicles and much papilla)

2) Conjunctival Complications

  • Xerosis: dryness of the conjunctiva and cornea due to scarring of the conjunctiva with obliteration and atrophy of goblet cells and accessory lacrimal glands.
  • Symblepharon: usually posterior symblepharon leading to shallow fornix.
  • Hyaline degeneration of the tarsus and conjunctiva.

3) Corneal Complications: Corneal scars in the form of:

  • Diffuse corneal nebulae resulting in irregular astigmatism.
  • Corneal leucoma.
  • Xerosis of the cornea.

4) Lacrimal complications

  • Chronic dacryocystitis.
  • Chronic dacryoadenitis.

Treatment of Trachoma

  • Prophylaxis: strict hygiene, good sanitation, combat of flies and health education.
  • Medical treatment: This is given only if there is active trachoma.
  • Surgical treatment: it is only needed in the following stages:
    • Stage T2a: Large mature follicles: These need to be expressed.
    • Stage T2b: Scraping of papillae is done using a sharp spoon
    • Stage T3 and T4: With PTDS and PTCS. Both need picking.
