Cataract · Congenital Types

Posterior Polar, Blue Dotted and Coronary Cataract

3) Posterior Polar Cataract:

Clinical picture:

Symptoms:

  • 1) The mother will notice marked drop of vision “Poor fixation” due to the proximity of the lens opacity to the nodal point.
  • 2) Rarely she will notice a white pupil “Leukocoria”, as these cases rarely progress to total cataract.

Signs:

  • 1) V.A. marked drop.
  • 2) Oblique illumination, only through a dilated pupil, will show a grayish white disc of opacity at the posterior pole of the lens.
  • 3) Slit lamp will show the same findings but under magnification and differentiate if it is a posterior polar or capsular cataract.
  • 4) Red reflex through a dilated pupil will show:
    • Peripheral bright red reflex through the clear periphery.
    • Central dim or blackened red reflex due to the central opacity.

4) Blue Dotted Cataract:

Symptoms: asymptomatic.

5) Coronary Cataract:

Symptoms: asymptomatic.

Signs: It manifested only at puberty.

  • 1) V.A. is unaffected.
  • 2) Oblique illumination and slit lamp examination on a fully dilated pupil will show:
    • a corona of peripheral club-shaped opacities.
  • 3) Red reflex on a fully dilated pupil:
    • Peripheral black oval spots corresponding to the opacities
