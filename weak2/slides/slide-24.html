Cornea · Dendritic ulcer

Typical features and complications

The typical dendritic ulcer has the following features:

  • Paracentral dendritic shape with the characteristic crenated edges. Irregular linear shape with numerous side branches each ending in a knob, with characteristic epitheliolysis of epithelium at the edges.
  • The ulcer is superficial, never perforates except after secondary infection or corticosteroid use.
  • There is corneal hypothesia due to affection of the nerves.
  • Defective vision as detected by the doctor.
  • Chronic course with tendency for recurrence as no solid immunity.

Complications:

Complications of the non-perforated ulcer:

  • Herpetic iridocyclitis
  • Secondary glaucoma: due to severe iridocyclitis and later on due to the synechia.
  • Disciform keratitis: It's due to virus antigen which diffuses down to the stroma and reacts with its antibodies leading. Clinically, there is central grey discoid opacity in the stroma with folds in Descemet's membrane.
  • Keratitis meta-herpetica: recurrent ulceration after primary healing but the virus can't be detected in the epithelium (it is due to marked corneal anesthesia).
  • Secondary bacterial infection: this may lead to amoeboid ulcer formation with hypopyon and even perforation.
  • Superficial corneal opacities: leaving paracentral nebulas.
