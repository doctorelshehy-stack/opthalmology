Cataract · 6) Rubella Total Cataract

General Lines for Treatment of Congenital Cataract

6) Rubella Total Cataract:

Cause:

  • Maternal rubella infection in the first trimester of pregnancy.

Clinical picture:

Symptoms:

  • The mother will notice the following:
  • 1) Defective vision “Poor fixation”.
  • 2) Leukocoria: white color of the pupil (usually present at birth).

Signs:

  • Systemic features of the congenital rubella syndrome: microcephaly, mental retardation, deafness, P.D.A., V.S.D... etc.
  • In the eye in addition to the cataract, micro-ophthalmos, buphthalmos, etc.
  • Cataract: starts as nuclear cataract but soon progresses to total opacification of the lens.
  • 1) V.A. is markedly dropped.
  • 2) Oblique illumination and slit lamp examination will show total opacification of the lens with no iris shadow.
  • 3) Red reflex is totally blackened.

Management depends on the density:

  • If the cataract is visually significant (dense or posterior polar):
    • Treatment is cataract extraction as early as possible.
  • If the cataract is not visually significant (not dense, peripheral or anterior polar):
    • Treatment is follow-up until the child is 5 years, in order to be able to tell about his V.A.

Type of surgery for the soft congenital cataract:

“Lensectomy and anterior vitrectomy”

It can be done through a pars plana approach or through a small corneal incision.
