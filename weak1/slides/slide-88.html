Part III · Orbital infection

Preseptal cellulitis

Definition

Infection of the eyelid and surrounding skin anterior to the orbital septum. Preseptal cellulitis is far more common than orbital cellulitis.

Etiology

Usually caused by contiguous spread of infection from local facial or eyelid injuries, insect or animal bites, conjunctivitis, chalazion, or sinusitis.

Clinical picture

  • Symptoms: swelling, pain, redness of the eyelid; sometimes fever.
  • Signs: tenderness, swelling, warmth, redness or discoloration of the eyelid; patients may be unable to open their eyes because of eyelid swelling.

Examination shows that visual acuity is not affected, ocular movement is intact and the globe is not pushed forward — very important to differentiate it from orbital cellulitis.

N.B. Diagnosis is mainly clinical. If the diagnosis is difficult, CT or MRI orbit can be done: soft tissue involvement is anterior to the orbital septum in preseptal cellulitis and posterior to it in orbital cellulitis. Treatment: same as orbital cellulitis.
