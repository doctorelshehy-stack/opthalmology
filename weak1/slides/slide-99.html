Operations of the orbit

1) Enucleation

Definition

Removal of the eyeball leaving the extraocular muscles, the conjunctiva and the optic nerve behind.

Indications

  • To stop pain in blind painful eyes, as in absolute glaucoma.
  • To save life, as in management of intraocular tumors (retinoblastoma).
  • To save the other eye, as in sympathetic ophthalmia.
  • To improve appearance in deformed eyes after trauma.

2) Evisceration

Definition

Removal of intraocular contents leaving the sclera, conjunctiva and the optic nerve free.

Indications

  • Endophthalmitis and panophthalmitis.
  • Expulsive hemorrhage.
Keeps the sphere The scleral shell preserves orbital volume and the motility of the prosthesis.

3) Exenteration

Definition

Removal of all orbital contents within the orbital periosteum, with the eyelids.

Indications

  • Malignant orbital tumors with free periosteum and no metastasis.
  • Malignant tumors of the lid, conjunctiva, and the eyeball.
