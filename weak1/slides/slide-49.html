Part I · Ptosis

Apparent or pseudoptosis

The upper lid is abnormally low although the levator muscle and Müller’s muscle are normal.

Causes

  • Enophthalmos.
  • Loss of orbital fat in old age.
  • Atrophic eye or empty socket (lack of support).
  • Unilateral lid retraction.

Clinical picture — symptoms

  • Bad cosmetic appearance.
  • Interference with vision (if the pupil is covered).

Signs

  • Drooping of the upper eyelid and the lid crease is absent.
  • In severe cases:
    • The frontalis muscle contracts to elevate the lid, leading to corrugations and wrinkling of the forehead and arching of the eyebrow.
    • The head is thrown backward in bilateral cases (chin elevation) and the head is tilted in unilateral cases.
