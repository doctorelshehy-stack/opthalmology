Part I · Ptosis

Acquired ptosis — less common

1) Neurogenic (paralytic)

  • 3rd nerve palsy, either compressive or vasculopathic.
  • Horner’s syndrome (sympathetic ptosis): mild unilateral ptosis, miosis and anhidrosis, enophthalmos, flushing of the face and heterochromia iridis (in congenital cases).

2) Myogenic

  • Disease of the levator muscle as in trauma, inflammation, neoplasms, muscle dystrophy or myasthenia gravis.
  • In myasthenia gravis the ptosis is periodic, worst in the evening when the patient is tired and disappearing in the morning.
  • Anticholinesterase drugs give rapid relief of ptosis in myasthenia gravis.

3) Aponeurotic (senile / involutional)

  • Degenerative changes in the levator muscle aponeurosis, while the levator function is good.

4) Traumatic

  • Direct trauma to the levator muscle or its nerve supply (wounds or fracture).

5) Mechanical

  • Increased weight of the lid by inflammatory infiltration, tumor, multiple chalazia, spring catarrh and blepharochalasis.

6) Hysterical

  • Occurs in a young female with emotional trouble.
