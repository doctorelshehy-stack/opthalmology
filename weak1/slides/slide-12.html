Part I · Muscles

Non-striated (involuntary) muscles

1) Müller’s muscle

  • Action: to sustain elevation of the upper eyelid.
  • Course: descends behind the levator aponeurosis.
  • Origin: posterior aspect of the levator muscle.
  • Insertion: the superior tarsal border.
N.B. It is innervated by autonomic sympathetic nerves, so its paralysis leads to Horner’s syndrome.

2) Retractors of the lower lid

In down-gaze the inferior rectus muscle retracts the lower lid through the lower lid retractors, which are attached to the inferior border of the tarsus of the lower lid, giving the lid its vertical stability.

Muscles of the eyelid — summary

  • Muscles of protraction: orbicularis oculi.
  • Muscles of retraction: upper lid — levator palpebrae superioris, Müller’s muscle; lower lid — capsulopalpebral fascia, inferior tarsal muscle.
  • Lower lid retractors are smooth muscles originating from the inferior rectus complex and receive sympathetic supply.
