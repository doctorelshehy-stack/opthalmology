Part I · Mal-directed lashes

Complications and treatment

Complications

  • Conjunctival: chronic conjunctivitis, conjunctival ulceration and epithelial plaque.
  • Corneal: recurrent ulceration which may be followed by opacification, superficial vascularization and epithelial plaque.

Rubbing lashes — treatment

  • Temporary: epilation — always followed by regrowth of stronger lashes within 2–3 weeks.
  • Permanent: destruction of the involved lashes by electrolysis, argon laser ablation, or diathermy and cryoapplication (may result in disfigurement and depigmentation of the lid margin).

Trichiasis — treatment

  • Pure trichiasis (without entropion) — treated surgically:
    • Removal of the lash-bearing area: indicated for partial-pure trichiasis.
    • Direct internal removal of the lash roots through an eyelid crease incision.
  • Trichiasis with entropion: treatment of the entropion.
Sequence epilation is a test of compliance and a temporizing measure — recurrence within weeks means a permanent destructive or surgical procedure is required.
