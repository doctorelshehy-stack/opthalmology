Part I · Ptosis

Ptosis — definition and congenital etiology

Definition

  • It is drooping of the upper eyelid.
  • Normally the upper lid covers the upper 1/6 of the cornea in the primary position.
Compared with the gross-anatomy note that the upper lid covers 1–2 mm of the cornea — the two descriptions express the same normal overlap.

A) Congenital — more common

  • Due to faulty development of the levator muscle or its nerve supply (levator muscle dysgenesis leading to poor contraction and incomplete relaxation).
  • Dated since birth, usually bilateral but may be unilateral, and may be associated with:
    • Weak superior rectus muscle — the superior rectus and levator develop from the same dermatome.
    • Other congenital anomalies as epicanthus and blepharophimosis.
    • Marcus Gunn phenomenon (jaw-winking) — the ptosed lid elevates on moving the jaw due to faulty innervation.
