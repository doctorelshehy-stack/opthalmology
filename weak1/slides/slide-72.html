Part II · Dacryocystitis

Chronic dacryocystitis — clinical picture

Clinical picture

  • Epiphora with discharge.
  • Inflammation and hyperemic inner canthus and caruncle.
  • Swelling of the lacrimal sac below the MPL.
  • Regurgitation test +ve: pressure on the lacrimal sac causes regurge of mucus or pus from either the puncta, or rarely down into the nose.

Investigations

As in epiphora.

Complications

  • Source of infection (septic focus) to any minute corneal abrasion that almost daily occurs → hypopyon corneal ulcer.
  • Continuous source of infection to any kind of intraocular surgery.
  • Epiphora causes eczema of the lids and ectropion.
  • Recurrent conjunctivitis.
  • Mucocele and pyocele.
  • Acute dacryocystitis, abscess and lacrimal fistula.
